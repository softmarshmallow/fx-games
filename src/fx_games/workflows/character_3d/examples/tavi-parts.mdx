The character is drawn twice: once whole, and once as a sheet of separate parts, the head, the hair
and the body, each from the front and the back. Every part becomes its own mesh, so each gets its
own texture and the hair stays a separate shell. The cost is putting them back together: an hour in
Blender matching scale and position, then a rig built by hand. FX Games holds both steps as one graph. An agent
fits the parts by placing each one whole, never reshaping it, then finds the joints, binds the skin
and bakes the test clips. A local Blender worker measures every hand-off, and a reviewer that did not
make the work judges each result at the size a player sees it.

The three meshes were made before the graph starts, one Tripo request per part from its two
drawings, and the graph picks them up from there. Doing the whole chain from a written brief in one
graph is experimental and is not shown.

## Where it works

<Scope>
  <Works>Adult characters drawn chibi-style, about two and a half heads tall</Works>
  <Works>Short hair generated as its own shell</Works>
  <Works>Mitten hands with a separate thumb: the fingers curl together and the thumb closes</Works>
  <Works>A scarf, collar or hood that covers where the head meets the body</Works>
  <Works>Games that show the character small on screen, around 120 pixels tall</Works>
  <Limit>A bare neck: the head and body overlap and are not welded, so the join has to be covered</Limit>
  <Limit>Long hair, ponytails and braids: hair is fixed to the head and does not move</Limit>
  <Limit>Animals, and characters carrying weapons, capes or props</Limit>
  <Limit>Facial expressions, separate fingers and cloth that moves on its own</Limit>
  <Tip>The export has a cheer and four test poses. Walk, run and idle come from your own animation library, retargeted onto its skeleton.</Tip>
  <Tip>The agent builds the rig itself here, which is why the hands close. The one-mesh workflow sends the mesh to a rig service and keeps the hands fixed.</Tip>
  <Tip>Check each back drawing against its front before meshing. Here the body's back was redrawn once: the first sheet put the pouch on the wrong side.</Tip>
  <Tip>Both rounds passed first time in this run. A refused fit or rig is repaired once and reviewed again, inside the same limit.</Tip>
</Scope>

## What goes wrong by hand

<Guards>
  <Guard node="assemble_01" how="A fit is one placement per part: a move, a turn and a size. The mesh and its texture are never edited.">Parts are sculpted to meet each other, and the texture smears where they were pulled.</Guard>
  <Guard node="assembly_review_01" how="A second pass that did not fit the parts checks the face from the front and the sides before any rig is built.">The hair sits low and covers the eyes, and nobody sees it until the character is in the game.</Guard>
  <Guard node="rig_review_01" how="The reviewer sees both hands enlarged, open and closed, and refuses a curl that goes the wrong way.">Fingers bend backwards, away from the palm.</Guard>
  <Guard node="rig_admit" how="The export is refused if any skinned vertex carries no weight, or if the reviewer accepted a different file.">A pouch or buckle is left unbound and stays behind when the body moves.</Guard>
  <Guard node="runtime_admit" how="Blender is checked before the agent starts.">A missing Blender feature turns up halfway through a paid fit.</Guard>
</Guards>
