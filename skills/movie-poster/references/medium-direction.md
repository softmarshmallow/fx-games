# Film-medium direction

Use this reference when the poster's medium is unspecified or when animation,
anime, stop motion, or a hybrid form materially changes the result.

## Selection

The user's explicit medium wins. If none is given, infer only when the synopsis
or intended film plainly supplies the answer; otherwise default to photographed
live action and state that choice. Do not treat photorealism as more mature or
more cinematic than animation.

Keep medium separate from genre and mood. `Anime`, `live-action photography`,
and `stop-motion puppetry` describe production media. `Melancholic`, `comic`,
and `thriller` do not.

## Photographed live action

The poster should look as though a production photographed performers, sets,
locations, wardrobe, and practical objects. Use
[live-action-realism.md](live-action-realism.md) for the relevant decisions and
review checks.

## Animated feature

Choose observable animation craft appropriate to the film: hand-drawn line and
paint, cut paper, stop-motion puppets, painted frames, graphic shapes, or a
specific 3D material and lighting approach. Preserve coherent character design,
background treatment, edge language, color scripting, and spatial rules across
the whole poster.

An animated poster should look like the theatrical identity of one finished
film, not a miscellaneous digital illustration. Do not add realism language,
camera defects, skin pores, or photographic material cues unless the chosen
animation deliberately simulates them.

## Literal anime

When the user asks for anime, honor it directly. Describe original Japanese
animation production traits that are visible in the desired result: 2D drawn
linework, character construction, cel or limited shading, painted or digitally
painted backgrounds, compositing, color design, and theatrical layout. Decide
whether the poster uses one scene, a character arrangement, or a graphic
one-sheet based on the film—not from a fixed anime template.

Keep characters, costumes, symbols, and settings original. Do not imitate a
named living artist, studio, protected franchise, or recognizable character.
Avoid the vague phrase `anime-inspired` when the user asked for literal anime;
also avoid drifting into plastic 3D character rendering unless requested.

## Stop motion and material animation

Name the physical construction: puppet scale, fabric, clay, paper, wood,
miniature sets, replacement faces, practical lighting, and the intended degree
of handmade imperfection. The materials must remain consistent rather than
turning into painted 2D art or photoreal human skin.

## Hybrids

Use a hybrid only when the source or user asks for one. State what belongs to
each medium and how they are composited. `Animated subject over photographed
location` is a decision; `cinematic mixed media` is not.

## Cross-medium review

Regardless of medium, verify that:

- the poster expresses the approved story without inventing canon;
- the medium is consistent and intentional across subjects and setting;
- composition has theatrical hierarchy rather than looking like a raw frame or
  generic illustration;
- title copy is exact and unwanted text is absent; and
- originality, anatomy or character construction, spatial logic, and material
  behavior survive inspection at original detail.
