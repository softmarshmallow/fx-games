A 47-tile set is what lets ground take any shape in a tile-based level: floors, floating ledges, steps, hollows and holes, with every edge and corner finished the right way. Painting one by hand means drawing all 47 variations so that each joins every other without a seam. FX Games holds that as a few steps of one graph. A locked template already has the right structure for every tile; the image model repaints it in the look of your scene in a single call, keeping each tile's finished sides exactly where they were. The sheet is then cut on fixed lines, checked for seams and holes, and packed into an atlas a game engine loads directly.

Where it works

Works well

  • A scene picture that shows the ground you want: paving, soil, rock, grass
  • Side-view levels on a square grid: floors, floating ledges, steps, hollows and holes
  • One material per atlas, with its own finish along the top
  • Engines that use 3×3 minimal terrain, such as Godot's terrain sets

Not expected to work

  • Smooth slopes: steps are square, and a slope needs its own tiles and collision
  • Two materials meeting, such as grass giving way to sand
  • Pixel art: the finish is hand-painted
  • Top-down maps

Good to know

  • The tiles are admitted by measurement, not by a reviewer. Draw a few shapes with them before you use them.
  • Busy ground shows its repeats. A course of slabs reads as a wall; a single bright pebble reads as a copy.
  • For now the atlas is made as part of a platformer's world preparation, which also paints that map's backgrounds.

What goes wrong by hand

By handIn the graphHow
Described in words, tile edges come back as the words: grooves for a grid, gaps where an underside is mentioned.The model repaints a template that already has every finished side and corner. It is never asked to draw structure from a description.
Tiles that look right alone show a line where they meet.Every join the tiles can make is measured, and a sheet with a visible step in tone is drawn again, within six tries.
A tile carries a hole or a strip of the guide line into the game.The sheet is cut on fixed lines inside the fence, and a tile with any transparent pixel is refused.
A level shape turns up that no tile was drawn for.The lookup of 47 neighbour patterns is fixed and checked complete, so every shape a level can take has a tile.

Lay out the level

A language model answers each map's terrain request with a grid of solid and empty cells. The atlas does not depend on it; the level preview does.

Paint over the template

One image call per map. The model is given the template's 48 cells behind a thin cyan fence, and the map's scene for its look. It repaints the art and keeps the structure. A sheet that fails the checks is drawn again, up to six tries.

Cut and check

Local and free. The sheet is cut on fixed lines into 120-pixel tiles, checked for holes and packed into the atlas, then laid out as the level to preview it.
