A side-scrolling level is drawn in layers: a sky, a far horizon, a midground, and a band in front of the player. The camera moves each one at its own speed, and that difference is what reads as depth. Every layer has to repeat without end, and a strip whose two ends do not meet shows a seam once every period. Mirroring the strip hides the seam and shows every landmark twice, facing itself. FX Games draws each layer from the map's reference picture and tests it as it came back. A layer that does not loop has its own wrap moved to the middle of a canvas the image model repaints, and the repaint is cut back in along the path where it agrees with the drawing. The wrap and both cuts are measured against the layer's own columns, and the layer is trimmed and placed for the game.

Try it

Make the scrolling backgrounds for a side-view platformer map from 1this picture, with FX Games' map layers described in godot/games/bellweather/docs/map-generation-contract.md. Start from a copy of the Bellweather package in godot/games/bellweather/inputs/default, put the picture in references/, and describe the map's layers far to near in its file under maps/, as maps/sunpetal-crossing.toml does, with loop_construction = "seam_repaint".

Plan it first. Then build the world part live, and show me every layer tiled across its wrap, with the path where each repaint was cut in.

The game's manifest carries each layer's plane, order, scroll speed, anchor and placement, so a game places and scrolls the layers without measuring anything.

Where it works

Works well

  • A side-scrolling level drawn in a few depth layers, far to near
  • Cut-out layers with transparency, and opaque backdrops
  • Any scroll speed, including a band in front that passes faster than the ground
  • A layer the model already draws as a loop, which is published untouched at no extra cost

Not expected to work

  • Loops across only; nothing repeats vertically
  • A landmark standing right on the wrap can be painted over by the repaint
  • A plain gradient: the model cannot repaint one to within a level of its own steps, so a gradient sky falls back to its reflection. An empty gradient reflected is the same picture, and these skies never move.

Good to know

  • Two of the six moving layers, the garden fence and the bell-tree route, came back already looping, and cost nothing more.
  • A join passes when it steps no more than the layer's own columns do. A fixed limit either misses a flat seam or refuses the detail of the art itself.
  • The reviewer passed both maps' looping, ground and layering. It still rejected them: Sunpetal lacks the bridge and stream its reference shows, and on both maps it judged the portal and ladder sheets at their full drawn size rather than as the game places them.
  • Each layer's size and height come from the game's own map files, so the far peaks stand behind the village and nothing shows between the layers even without the ground.

Sunpetal Crossing

Four layers, far to near, each drawn from the map's reference picture. Each one is tested as it came back; one that does not loop has its wrap repainted and cut back in. Then it is trimmed to its content and placed by its anchor. Last, a board of every layer and the ground is judged by a model that drew none of it; its verdict is advice, and does not stop the run.
