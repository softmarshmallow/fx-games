The character is drawn twice: once whole, and once as a sheet of separate parts, the head, the hair and the body, each from the front and the back. Every part becomes its own mesh, so each gets its own texture and the hair stays a separate shell. The cost is putting them back together: an hour in Blender matching scale and position, then a rig built by hand. FX Games holds both steps as one graph. An agent fits the parts by placing each one whole, never reshaping it, then finds the joints, binds the skin and bakes the test clips. A local Blender worker measures every hand-off, and a reviewer that did not make the work judges each result at the size a player sees it.

The three meshes were made before the graph starts, one Tripo request per part from its two drawings, and the graph picks them up from there. Doing the whole chain from a written brief in one graph is experimental and is not shown.

Where it works

Works well

  • Adult characters drawn chibi-style, about two and a half heads tall
  • Short hair generated as its own shell
  • Mitten hands with a separate thumb: the fingers curl together and the thumb closes
  • A scarf, collar or hood that covers where the head meets the body
  • Games that show the character small on screen, around 120 pixels tall

Not expected to work

  • A bare neck: the head and body overlap and are not welded, so the join has to be covered
  • Long hair, ponytails and braids: hair is fixed to the head and does not move
  • Animals, and characters carrying weapons, capes or props
  • Facial expressions, separate fingers and cloth that moves on its own

Good to know

  • The export has a cheer and four test poses. Walk, run and idle come from your own animation library, retargeted onto its skeleton.
  • The agent builds the rig itself here, which is why the hands close. The one-mesh workflow sends the mesh to a rig service and keeps the hands fixed.
  • Check each back drawing against its front before meshing. Here the body's back was redrawn once: the first sheet put the pouch on the wrong side.
  • Both rounds passed first time in this run. A refused fit or rig is repaired once and reviewed again, inside the same limit.

What goes wrong by hand

Three meshes

Made before the graph starts: one Tripo request per part, from the front and back drawings cut from the parts sheet. The graph picks the files up by digest.

Before the agent is paid

Blender's required features are checked offline. The agent does not start until they pass.

Measure

Each mesh is converted and measured as it arrived. Nothing is moved or fitted yet.

Fit

An agent places the head on the body and the hair on the head. Each part gets one placement: a move, a turn and a size. A reviewer that did not fit them judges the exported result. Round two runs only after a refusal.

Skeleton and clips

The agent finds the joints on the fitted mesh, binds the skin and bakes the test clips. The reviewer sees every pose from five sides, with the hands enlarged.

Admission

The export is admitted only if the reviewer accepted this exact file and every skinned vertex carries weights.
